# RuleScale Media — Blog & Content Brief

Last updated: 2026-08-08

This is a business/marketing brief, not a technical one — written for whoever is writing blog
content for this site. It explains what RuleScale Media is, who it's for, how it wants to sound,
and how to think about SEO and AEO (Answer Engine Optimization) when writing. For "how the site is
built," see `AGENT.md`/`systemstatus.md` instead — none of that matters for this document.

---

## 1. What RuleScale Media is, in one paragraph

RuleScale Media is a full-service marketing agency. Clients hire it to make their brand actually
grow — not just look better. That means website design and development, SEO, paid/performance
advertising, social media, influencer marketing, video production, content marketing, brand
strategy, multi-lingual/localized marketing, and the analytics to prove any of it worked. The
pitch isn't "we do marketing" — every agency says that. The pitch is: most agencies sell
*activity* (posts published, ads launched, reports sent); RuleScale sells the moment a customer
actually remembers the brand and comes back. That's the tagline: **"We grow brands people
remember."**

## 2. Who this is for

The reader (and the client) is usually a founder, marketing lead, or small in-house marketing team
at a growing business — not a Fortune 500 CMO with a 40-person internal team. Real client work so
far spans **e-commerce** (a fashion/lifestyle brand and an artisanal honey brand both rebuilt as
conversion-focused e-commerce sites) and **AI/SaaS** (a photo/creative tool platform). The stated
target range is broad on purpose — DTC, hospitality, SaaS, retail, professional services — but the
common thread in every piece of site copy is: **someone who has been burned by an agency before.**

They've usually already tried one of these, and it didn't work:
- A freelancer or agency that made things "prettier" but conversion didn't move.
- A big-name agency with a slick pitch deck, then radio silence and vague monthly reports.
- Posting content/ads without a strategy behind them — activity with no compounding result.

That skepticism is the emotional starting point for almost every piece of content. Don't write to
someone who's never worked with an agency and is starry-eyed about marketing — write to someone
who's a little burned, watching their budget, and wants proof before they believe another pitch.

## 4. Brand voice for blog writing

**The single test for any paragraph: would this survive being said out loud to a skeptical
founder, or does it sound like agency-speak?** If it sounds like the latter, cut it.

Do:
- Lead with a specific, concrete claim or number, not a vague generalization.
- Write like you're explaining it to a smart friend who runs a business, not pitching a boardroom.
- Take a clear position. "It depends" is true but useless — say what you'd actually do and why.
- Name the objection before the reader thinks it. If a tactic is slow, say so, then say why it's
  still worth it.
- Keep paragraphs short. Whitespace and directness are part of the brand's actual visual design
  philosophy (premium editorial minimalism) — the writing should feel the same way.
- Use real numbers wherever possible, even approximate honest ones, over adjectives like
  "amazing" or "incredible."

Don't:
- Corporate/agency filler: "leverage synergies," "holistic 360° approach," "unlock your brand's
  potential," "in today's fast-paced digital landscape." None of it. Ever.
- Generic listicle padding ("10 Tips for Social Media Success!") with no actual point of view.
- Overpromising ("guaranteed #1 ranking," "go viral overnight"). The brand's whole positioning is
  *honesty about timelines and effort* — see the FAQ answers below for the exact tone to match.
- Lorem-ipsum-flavored filler sentences that exist just to hit a word count. If a paragraph isn't
  saying something specific, delete it.

## 6. Why this blog exists

Three jobs, in priority order:

1. **Organic acquisition (SEO).** The blog is meant to be the site's #1 long-term acquisition
   channel — the same story as the real client case study where a content/SEO rebuild became a
   brand's top acquisition channel. Every post should be built to actually rank, not just exist.
2. **Answer-engine visibility (AEO).** Increasingly, people don't click through ten search results
   — they ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google's AI Overview a direct question and read the summary.
   Posts need to be *quotable*: a clear, direct, correctly-scoped answer near the top, so an AI
   summarizer can lift it cleanly and (ideally) attribute it back to RuleScale.
3. **Pre-sold trust.** By the time a skeptical reader lands on `/contact`, a good blog has already
   done a chunk of the sales conversation — proven the agency actually knows the subject, isn't
   vague about timelines, and isn't hiding behind buzzwords. Every post should end with a natural,
   low-pressure next step (not a hard sell) — usually a link toward the relevant service section on
   the homepage or `/contact`.

## 7. SEO basics for every post

- **Search intent first.** Before writing, decide: is this an *informational* post (someone
  learning "what is X" or "how does X work") or a *commercial* one (someone comparing options
  before hiring)? Informational posts should teach generously with no hard sell; commercial-intent
  posts can be more direct about RuleScale's approach and results.
- **One clear primary keyword/question per post**, matched to what someone would actually type or
  ask — not a vague topic. "How long does SEO take to show results?" beats "SEO Timelines."
- **Answer the core question in the first 2–3 sentences.** Don't bury the point under a long
  intro — this matters for both human skimmers and AI summarizers (see AEO below).
- **Internal link on purpose**, not by habit: link to the specific homepage service section, a
  real `/work` case study, or `/contact` when it's genuinely the natural next step for the reader
  — never a generic "click here."
- **Titles and meta descriptions should read like a real, specific answer**, not a vague teaser —
  matches how the rest of the site's SEO metadata is written (specific, benefit-led, no clickbait).

## 8. AEO — writing for AI answer engines, not just Google

This is newer and matters more every month. AI assistants pull short, well-structured, directly
sourced answers — not entire pages. Practical rules:

- **Front-load the answer.** State the direct answer to the implied question in the first
  paragraph, in plain language, before any setup or story. Elaborate *after*, not before.
- **Use question-shaped subheadings** (H2/H3) where it's natural — "How much does SEO cost?" is a
  better subheading than "Pricing Considerations." It mirrors how people actually phrase questions
  to an AI assistant, and how that assistant is likely to phrase the summarized answer back.
- **Be specific and self-contained per section.** Each H2 section should make sense pulled out of
  context on its own — an AI summarizer will often lift one section, not the whole article.
- **Numbers, ranges, and named specifics beat vague qualifiers.** "Most engagements start between
  $3,000–$15,000/month" is answer-engine gold. "Pricing varies depending on your needs" is not.
- **Lists and short structured answers outrank long unbroken prose** for this purpose — a
  3–5 item bulleted answer to "what should I look for in an SEO agency" is exactly the shape an
  answer engine wants to extract.
- **The site's own FAQ section is the existing gold standard** — every answer there is direct,
  specific, and honest about tradeoffs (see below). Match that tone and structure in blog posts.

## 9. Existing FAQ — ready-made AEO targets and tone reference

These are already live on the homepage and are the clearest example of the voice and directness
every blog post should match. They're also genuinely good starting points for standalone,
deeper blog posts (each could become a full article that the homepage FAQ then links out to):

- **"What does RuleScale Media actually specialize in?"** — Website design & development, SEO,
  social and influencer marketing, video and content production, programmatic and performance
  advertising, and the brand strategy that ties it together. Most clients start with one channel
  and expand once it's proven out.
- **"How long until we see results?"** — Paid channels: early signal in 2–4 weeks. SEO and
  content: compounds over 3–6 months. Always give the honest timeline, not the optimistic one.
- **"Do you work with our industry?"** — DTC, hospitality, SaaS, retail, professional services.
  What matters more than industry is a real offer and willingness to move fast.
- **"What makes RuleScale different?"** — Small enough that clients talk to the people doing the
  work; disciplined enough to report real numbers instead of vanity metrics. No year-long
  contracts, no black-box reporting.
- **"How much should we budget?"** — $3,000–$15,000/month for services (separate from ad spend),
  scoped to actual goals rather than a generic package.
- **"What's the contract length?"** — Month-to-month after a 90-day onboarding period.

## 11. Quick checklist before publishing any post

1. Does the first paragraph directly answer the implied question, no throat-clearing?
2. Is there at least one specific number, named example, or concrete detail — not just adjectives?
3. Would this survive being read out loud to a skeptical founder without sounding like agency-speak?
4. Does it link somewhere real and useful (a service section, a `/work` case study, `/contact`) —
   not a generic CTA?
5. Could an AI assistant lift one section cleanly as a self-contained answer?
